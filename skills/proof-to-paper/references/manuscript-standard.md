# Manuscript standard

Read this reference after the paperization entry checks pass and before
outlining or drafting the manuscript.

## Sources and structure

Use one authoritative bibliography and make the manuscript compile
independently. Let the approved theorem scope, proof dependencies, venue, and
reader needs determine the structure. Include a table of contents, notation
table, dependency map, or appendix only when it materially improves navigation
or satisfies a venue requirement.

## Manuscript architecture

Order the exposition by proof dependency, placing supporting results before
their use when practical. Avoid sections whose size or nesting hides the
decisive mathematical steps.

At a level proportionate to the manuscript format, the introduction must make
clear:

- the precise problem and variational setting;
- the closest literature and the exact unresolved point;
- the new results, with assumptions and scope visible;
- the main theorem statements;
- a proportionate account of the proof architecture.

Use result-driven titles and make clear what each section proves and where that
result is used. Separate logically independent arguments. Remove discovery
chronology, audit terminology, internal workflow labels, and obsolete failed
approaches unless they carry genuine mathematical explanation.

With a current proof-refactor handoff, preserve its proof spine, lemma
dispositions, technical boundary, and terminology cleanup unless
self-containedness or venue requirements demand a change. Verify every
mathematical adjustment against the canonical source.

## Writing standard

Use the language required by the user or venue. Otherwise preserve the dominant
language of the approved source material.

- Put correctness, exact scope, local verifiability, and venue requirements
  before brevity. Prefer the simplest complete proof architecture, not merely
  shorter prose.
- Among mathematically complete routes, prefer the one with the least auxiliary
  notation, intermediate lemmas, case splitting, and repetition. Never omit a
  load-bearing step or hide it behind “standard.”
- Use precise, plain mathematical language. Let definitions, estimates,
  identities, and formulas carry the argument; do not propagate mnemonic IDs,
  coined workflow labels, or discovery-history terminology into the prose.
- A technique name is not a proof. State the formula, sign structure,
  hypotheses, and conclusion that make every load-bearing use work.
- Delete filler, hype, conversational asides, repeated scope defenses, and
  unsupported claims of obviousness, exactness, or sharpness.
- Define every symbol before use. Quantify variables and constants. Keep
  conventions, orientations, gauges, and scaling factors explicit.
- State each assumption once and track its use. Do not add assumptions for
  convenience.
- Give complete support for load-bearing estimates. Do not hide a new
  compactness, coercivity, matching, regularity, or liminf argument behind a
  technique name.
- Use primary, authoritative references. Verify metadata, theorem locations,
  hypotheses, and the claim supported by each citation.

When the user requests calibration to a named author or venue, inspect a small
representative set of authoritative examples and extract structural traits
rather than wording.
